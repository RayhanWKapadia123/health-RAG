## Overview

COVID-19 (coronavirus disease 2019) is a disease caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus. It can be very contagious and can spread quickly via droplets released into the air. COVID-19 most often causes respiratory symptoms that can feel much like a cold, the flu, or pneumonia. COVID-19 can attack more than your lungs and respiratory system. Other parts of your body can also be affected by the disease. Most people with COVID-19 have mild symptoms, but some people become severely ill. People who are up to date with their COVID-19 vaccine have lower risk of severe illness. Practice good hygiene by covering coughs and sneezes, washing or sanitizing hands often, and cleaning frequently touched surfaces.

### Frequently Asked Questions

Adults and children may have some side effects from a COVID-19 vaccine, including pain, redness or swelling at the injection site, tiredness, headache, muscle pain, chills, fever, and nausea. These side effects typically resolve after a few days. [Serious side effects](https://www.cdc.gov/coronavirus/2019-ncov/vaccines/safety/adverse-events.html) are rare but may occur.

- CDC recommends everyone ages 65 years and older, including people who live and work in LTC settings, get 2 doses of a COVID-19 vaccine 6 months apart.
- People who are moderately or severely immunocompromised should get at least 2 doses of a COVID-19 vaccine 6 months apart. They may also get more age-appropriate doses, beyond two doses at least 2 months apart, after talking to a healthcare provider.

- People can self-confirm as moderately or severely immunocompromised. This means they do NOT need documentation to receive a COVID-19 vaccination they are eligible for.
- While it is the *recommended* to get a COVID-19 vaccine dose 6 months apart, the *minimum* time is 2 months apart, which allows flexibility to get the second dose prior to typical COVID-19 surges, travel, life events, and healthcare visits.

**COVID-19 vaccination during pregnancy helps:**

- Prevent severe illness and death in women who are pregnant.
- [Protect babies younger than 6 months old](https://www.cdc.gov/covid/vaccines/pregnant-or-breastfeeding.html) from hospitalization.

Learn more about vaccination considerations and the [safety and effectiveness of COVID-19 vaccines](https://www.cdc.gov/covid/vaccines/pregnant-or-breastfeeding.html#cdc_vaccine_basics_who-safety-and-effectiveness-of-covid-19-vaccination-during-pregnancy) during pregnancy or while breastfeeding.

There is no recommended waiting period between getting a [COVID-19 vaccine and other vaccines](https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/covid-19/clinical-considerations/covid-19-vaccines-us.html). You can get a COVID-19 vaccine and other vaccines, including a [flu vaccine](https://www.cdc.gov/flu/vaccines/index.html), at the same visit.

While most children and teens with no underlying conditions have mild COVID-19 illness, some can still experience more severe illness, hospitalization, and death. Getting [children and teens vaccinated against COVID-19](https://www.cdc.gov/covid/vaccines/stay-up-to-date.html) can help keep them from getting seriously ill if they do get COVID-19, including protecting them from short- and long-term complications and hospitalization.

Learn more about the [benefits of getting a COVID-19 vaccine](https://www.cdc.gov/covid/vaccines/benefits.html).

COVID-19 vaccines help prevent disease, hospitalization, and death. Unvaccinated people are [more likely](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/70/wr/mm7032e1.htm) to get COVID-19, require hospitalization and serious infection including death from COVID-19, compared to people who are [up to date](https://www.cdc.gov/covid/vaccines/stay-up-to-date.html) with their COVID-19 vaccinations.
