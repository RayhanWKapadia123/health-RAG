# Amblyomma americanum

*Health > IDEPD > Vector-Borne and Zoonotic Disease > Vector-Borne Diseases > tick-borne-diseases > Amblyomma americanum*

**Common name**: Lone Star tick

##### Figure 1: Lone Star ticks

[image: Lone Star Ticks.png]

*Photo: Adult female, adult male, nymph lone star ticks, Indiana Department of Health*

Lone Star ticks are widely distributed in the eastern United States, but more common in the South. These ticks are most often encountered in brushy areas. Click [here](https://www.cdc.gov/ticks/about/where-ticks-live.html?CDC_AAref_Val=https://www.cdc.gov/ticks/geographic_distribution.html#:~:text=map.A-,Lone%20star%20tick%20(Amblyomma%20americanum),-Amblyomma%20americanum) to see the geographic distribution of ticks that bite humans in the United States.

To see where Lone Star ticks have been detected, visit the [Indiana Tick-borne Disease Surveillance dashboard](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/vector-borne-diseases/tick-borne-diseases) and select "Ehrlichiosis" from the disease dropdown menu.

##### Figure 2: Lone Star tick habitat

[image: lone star habitat]

*Photo: Lone star tick habitat, Indiana Department of Health*

All life stages of Lone Star ticks can feed on humans. The Lone Star tick life cycle consists of four stages (egg, larva, nymph and adult) and usually takes two years to complete. These ticks are known to be aggressive feeders and will pursue many different species of animals for a blood meal. Most Lone Star tick activity occurs in spring and summer. Understanding seasonal activity, or phenology, of vector species is important to determining seasonality of disease risk.

[image: LST phenology.png]

*Phenology chart of Amblyomma americanum. Graphic: Indiana Department of Health.*

- [Ehrlichiosis](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/zoonotic-diseases/ehrlichiosis)
- [Tularemia](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/zoonotic-diseases/tularemia)
- [Heartland virus](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/zoonotic-diseases/heartland-virus)
- [Bourbon virus](https://www.cdc.gov/bourbon-virus/about/)
- [Southern tick-associated rash illness (STARI)](https://www.cdc.gov/stari/about/?CDC_AAref_Val=https://www.cdc.gov/lyme/about/about-southern-tick-associated-rash-illness.html)
- [Alpha-gal Syndrome](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/vector-borne-diseases/alpha-gal-syndrome) is an allergic condition associated with the bite of a Lone Star tick.

The best way to prevent diseases associated with Lone Star ticks is to avoid tick bites. Please see our [tick prevention page](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/vector-borne-diseases/tick-borne-diseases/tick-bite-prevention) for more information.

Please visit the [Midwest Center of Excellence in Vector-Borne Disease webpage](http://mcevbd.wisc.edu/ticks/lone-star-tick) for more information about Lone Star ticks.
