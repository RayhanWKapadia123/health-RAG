# Ixodes scapularis

*Health > IDEPD > Vector-Borne and Zoonotic Disease > Vector-Borne Diseases > tick-borne-diseases > Ixodes scapularis*

**Common name**: Blacklegged tick

[image: Graphic: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.]

*Graphic: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.*

Blacklegged ticks are present in most Indiana counties and widely distributed in the Northeastern and upper Midwestern United States. These ticks are most encountered in wooded environments. Click [here](https://www.cdc.gov/ticks/about/where-ticks-live.html?CDC_AAref_Val=https://www.cdc.gov/ticks/geographic_distribution.html#:~:text=on%20an%20animal.-,Blacklegged%20tick%20(Ixodes%20scapularis),-Ixodes%20scapularis) to see the geographic distribution of ticks that bite humans in the United States.

To see where blacklegged ticks have been detected, visit the [Indiana Tick-borne Disease Surveillance dashboard](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/vector-borne-diseases/tick-borne-diseases) and select "Lyme disease" from the disease dropdown menu.

In Indiana, nymphs are most active during spring and summer and adults are most active during the fall and early spring. However, adult ticks may be active any time winter temperatures are above freezing. The blacklegged tick life cycle consists of four stages (egg, larva, nymph, and adult) and usually takes two years to complete. At each stage, the tick must have a blood meal in order to molt and develop to the next stage. Both nymphs and adults can feed on humans and are capable of transmitting disease.

- [Lyme disease](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/zoonotic-diseases/lyme-disease)
- [Anaplasmosis](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/zoonotic-diseases/anaplasmosis)
- [Babesiosis](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/zoonotic-diseases/babesiosis)
- [Powassan virus](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/zoonotic-diseases/powassan-virus)

The best way to prevent diseases associated with blacklegged ticks is to avoid tick bites. Please see our [tick prevention page](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/vector-borne-diseases/tick-borne-diseases/tick-bite-prevention)for more information.

For more information about blacklegged ticks, please visit the [Midwest Center of Excellence in Vector-Borne Disease](http://mcevbd.wisc.edu/ticks/deer-tick).
