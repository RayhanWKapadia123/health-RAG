### Reporting Requirements

1. Indiana statute (410 IAC 29-3-1) requires that **ALL**blood lead tests, **REGARDLESS OF RESULTS** are required to be reported to IDOH by the entity examining the specimen (i.e. Laboratory, Clinic, Physician, etc.) not later than one (1) week after completing the test.
2. The IDOH Lead and Healthy Homes Division can accept blood lead level testing results through HL7 messaging OR direct entry into the Lead Data Flow (LDF) database and the Children and Hoosier Immunization Registry Program (CHIRP)

##### Direct Entry:

1. **Lead Data Flow (LDF):**Blood lead level test results can be entered directly into LDF electronic gateway portal. An account will be created for each person that reports test results. See below for instructions regarding creating an account.
2. **[Children and Hoosier Immunization Registry Program](https://www.in.gov/health/immunization/children-and-hoosiers-immunization-registry-program-chirp)**(CHIRP): Providers can enter blood lead test results directly into CHIRP as a means for submitting results to IDOH. Entered test results remain editable after submission. Contact the CHIRP Help Desk at 1-888-227-4439 for problems accessing CHIRP. Contact Scile Wilz at swilz@health.in.gov with other questions.

- Options and instructions for signing up for electronic reporting into LDF and through HL7 messaging are found [here.](https://www.in.gov/health/leadsafe/files/newIDOH-Lead-Data-Reporting-Options.pdf)
- Contact Hazarath Thanneeru (hthanneeru@isdh.in.gov) (hthanneeru@health.in.gov) with questions about blood lead level reporting or signing up for electronic reporting.

IDOH can also accept test results through secure fax or secure email, but this method is **not recommended**as the best option for submitting blood lead level testing results and should be considered a temporary or short-term option for submitting results. Per 410 IAC 29-3-1(b) any provider or lab submitting **more than 50 results per year will be required to report electronically**.

### Testing Requirements

- **Universal Screening (Testing):**On March 10, 2022, Governor Holcomb signed House Enrolled Act (HEA) 1313 into law requiring health care providers to confirm that children under 7 have been tested for lead, and if not, to offer this testing to the parent or guardian of that child. Children should receive a blood lead test between the ages of nine (9) and fifteen (15) months, or as close as reasonably possible, and again between twenty-one (21) and twenty-seven (27) months of age, or as close as reasonably possible. Any child between twenty-eight (28) and seventy-two (72) months with no record of a previous blood lead test must have one as soon as possible.
- **Medicaid Recipient Testing:**Indiana Medicaid requires all children be tested for lead according to the latest [EPSDT schedule](https://www.in.gov/medicaid/providers/files/modules/epsdt.pdf). Currently, the required testing intervals match with the Universal Screening periodicity outline in the bullet above.
- **Refugee Infants and Children Testing:**All refugee infants and children up to age 16, particularly refugee and internationally adopted children, should be evaluated with a blood test for lead exposure at the domestic medical screening visit. Repeat blood lead testing for all refugee infants and children >= 6 years of age, regardless of initial screening BLL results is to be done within 3-6 months after initial testing.

### Risk Assessment

Indiana statute, 410 IAC 29-1-6, requires that local health departments provide an environmental inspection, including a risk assessment of the child’s primary and secondary addresses for properties built before 1978 and an environmental investigation, for children with confirmed elevated blood lead levels at or above 5 µg/dL.

**Lead Risk Assessor Training:**

Only Indiana-licensed lead risk assessors may conduct lead risk assessments. Indiana statute, 410 IAC 32-2-3, outlines the licensing requirements for risk assessors.

To obtain an Indiana lead risk assessor license, an individual must:

1. Possess a qualifying combination of education and experience.
2. Complete an approved lead inspector and risk assessor course with an [approved training provider](https://www.in.gov/health/leadsafe/lead-information-contractors/#Where_do_I_get_training_).
3. Pass [third-party examinations](https://www.in.gov/health/leadsafe/lead-information-contractors/#How_do_I_apply_for_a_license_) for lead inspector and lead risk assessor disciplines.
4. Apply for licensure by emailing a completed [State Form 49240](https://www.in.gov/health/leadsafe/lead-information-contractors/#How_do_I_apply_for_a_license_) to Marcia Jordan at majordan@health.in.gov. Local health department staff should apply for licensure this way instead of applying online through the Indiana Professional Licensing Agency.

Individuals interested in becoming lead risk assessors may request to shadow an IDOH risk assessor. To do so, they should reach out to their county’s [IDOH environmental staff contact](https://www.in.gov/dA/4493f322bb/LeadEnviro-Field-Staff-Map-as-of-9.16.25.pdf?language_id=1). Once an individual is licensed as a risk assessor, they may also request that an IDOH risk assessor shadow them on their first risk assessment.

If a local health department has questions or concerns about who can conduct risk assessments in their area, please reach out to Katie Etter at ketter@health.in.gov.

### Abatement vs. Renovation

Indiana statute, 410 IAC 29-4-1, states that local health officers can order what is reasonable and necessary to prevent an elevated blood lead level or remediate lead hazards. Once lead hazards have been identified by a risk assessor, the risk assessor should recommend interim control and abatement options to remediate each hazard.

- Abatement is any set of measures designed to permanently eliminate lead-based paint hazards. These measures include removal of lead-based paint, enclosure, encapsulation, replacement of building components, and other permanent measures to eliminate lead-based paint hazards.
- While abatement is the end goal, lead hazards can be immediately reduced through interim controls, measures designed to temporarily reduce human exposure to lead-based paint hazards. These measures include dust removal, paint film stabilization, treatment of friction and impact surfaces, covering soil, and land use controls. Interim controls are not long-term solutions to lead hazards and lead hazards should be regularly monitored and managed until abatement is possible.

**Abatement Resources**

- Homeowners and renters may qualify for financial assistance for lead abatement in their home. More information about the Indiana Housing & Community Development Authority (IHCDA) program and application can be found [here](https://www.in.gov/ihcda/homeowners-and-renters/lead-protection-program). More information about the Improving Kids Environment (IKE) program and application can be found [here](https://www.ikecoalition.org/healthy-homes/lead-health-services-initiative).
- Homeowners should only work with Indiana-licensed lead abatement contractors to perform lead abatement. Lead abatement contractors can be searched [here](https://mylicense.in.gov/everification/Search.aspx?facility=Y).

Not all work done on homes that results in the elimination of lead hazards is considered abatement. Repair, renovation and painting activities in pre-1978 homes may result in the disturbance of lead even though that is not the purpose of the activities. These non-abatement activities are regulated under EPA’s Lead Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) rule, which you can read about [here](https://www.epa.gov/lead/lead-renovation-repair-and-painting-program). RRP workers need to use lead-safe work practices and licensed contractors can be searched for [here](https://www.epa.gov/lead/how-can-i-find-certified-renovation-firm-my-area).
