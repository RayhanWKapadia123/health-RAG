# Rhipicephalus sanguineus

*Health > IDEPD > Vector-Borne and Zoonotic Disease > Vector-Borne Diseases > tick-borne-diseases > Rhipicephalus sanguineus*

**Common name:** Brown dog tick

Present in all Indiana counties and widely distributed throughout the United States. Click [here](https://www.cdc.gov/ticks/about/where-ticks-live.html?CDC_AAref_Val=https://www.cdc.gov/ticks/geographic_distribution/guldcoasttick.html#cdc_generic_section_4-brown-dog-tick-rhipicephalus-sanguineus:~:text=map.B-,Brown%20dog%20tick%20(Rhipicephalus%20sanguineus),-Rhipicephalus%20sanguineus) to see the geographic distribution of ticks that bite humans in the United States.

This is the only tick in Indiana that can complete its lifecycle indoors. It is often associated with pets and indoor environments, especially dog kennels. The brown dog tick can survive very well indoors as well as outdoors. Its life cycle consists of four stages (egg, larva, nymph, and adult). This tick is unique in that all its life stages can feed on the same host species (dogs), but the tick may also bite humans or other animals.

[image: Graphic: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.]

*Graphic: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.*

- [Rocky Mountain Spotted Fever](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/zoonotic-diseases/rocky-mountain-spotted-fever) is transmitted by brown dog ticks in the southwestern U.S. and along the U.S.-Mexico border, but not in Indiana.

The best way to prevent diseases associated with brown dog ticks is to avoid tick bites. Please see our [tick prevention page](https://www.in.gov/health/idepd/zoonotic-and-vectorborne-epidemiology-entomology/vector-borne-diseases/tick-borne-diseases/tick-bite-prevention) for more information.
